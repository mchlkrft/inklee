The live document is `apps/web/content/legal/terms.md`, version-gated by
`getLegalDoc()` (`apps/web/src/lib/legal/documents.ts`): a version bump
requires a frozen snapshot under `content/legal/_versions/{version}/terms.md`,
and `terms_approved` (a `billing_activation_approvals` key) is bound to the
resulting `versionHash` — editing the live file without going through that
snapshot workflow fails CI by design and does not move the approval. **Do not
edit `terms.md` from this document; that edit happens once, in the same pass
as everything below, through the versioned workflow, with counsel's final
render pass on the actual hash.**

This bundles eight items. Five (C1.1, C1.2, C1.3, C1.8, C1.4) are **already
implemented in the checkout UI and confirmation emails** — the wording below
matches what a buyer sees today. What is still missing is folding a POLICY
SUMMARY of those into the Terms document itself (the document a buyer never
reads at checkout but that governs the relationship), plus two Terms-only
items that have no UI counterpart (C1.7, A5) and one pure wording fix (X2).

## Suggested structure

The current Terms (`terms.md`) has no section for standalone goods orders at
all — Section 12 ("Deposits and payments") only covers deposits. Suggested:
insert a **new Section 13, "Goods orders"**, modelled on Section 12's own
shape ("You are the seller / merchant of record..."), renumbering the current
13–20 to 14–21. The A5 line fits naturally as a bullet inside that new
section (or Section 6, "Your responsibility as an Artist" — either works;
Section 13 keeps every goods-related obligation in one place, so this
document defaults to that). X2 is an unrelated one-line fix elsewhere in
Section 11 and rides along in the same version bump only because the process
batches Terms edits.

## C1.2 — Return-right wording, postage, the custom-made exemption

**Approved wording** (handoff PART 4, C1.2):

> **Right of return.** You may withdraw from this purchase within 14 days of
> the day you (or someone you nominate) receive the goods, without giving a
> reason. To do so, tell the artist ([artist contact]) or Inklee
> ([support email]) in a clear statement before the period ends; you may use
> the model withdrawal form [link/attached]. Send the goods back to the
> artist within 14 days of telling us. **You bear the direct cost of
> returning the goods.** The refund — including standard delivery cost, if
> you paid one — is made within 14 days of your withdrawal, though it may be
> withheld until the goods are back or you prove you sent them. You are
> liable only for any diminished value from handling beyond what a shop
> would allow.
>
> **Custom-made item: no right of return.** This item is made to your
> specification or clearly personalised, so the 14-day right of return does
> not apply (Art. 16(c), Consumer Rights Directive).

Claimed **per product**, never in the Terms alone (an undisclosed or blanket
claim fails, and extends the withdrawal window to 12 months under Art. 10).

**Already implemented**: `returnRightNotice()` and `CUSTOM_MADE_NOTICE` in
`packages/shared/src/consumer-disclosures.ts`, rendered in `shop-checkout.tsx`
(product/cart/checkout lines) and in the confirmation email. The `custom_made`
boolean lives on `products` (migration `0142_goods_consumer_disclosures.sql`)
and is snapshotted per line (`order_items.custom_made_snapshot`) so a later
catalog edit never changes what a past buyer was shown. Section 13 gets a
short POLICY-LEVEL summary (buyer-facing exact wording stays in the checkout
page and email, per counsel's "never in the Terms alone" instruction):

> Buyers of physical goods have a 14-day right of return except for
> custom-made or clearly personalised items, as disclosed on the product and
> at checkout. You are responsible for honouring return requests and refunds
> on goods orders you sell, consistent with the return-right and custom-made
> disclosures Inklee shows the buyer on your behalf.

## C1.7 — Terms clause for the retained processing cost

**Approved wording** (handoff PART 4, C1.7) — **NOT YET in the Terms; this is
the one item with no existing home anywhere in `terms.md`**:

> **Refunds of our fee.** If you cancel and refund a payment, we return the
> platform fee we charged on it, less the card-processing cost we were
> charged by our payment provider for that payment and cannot recover. We
> retain only the amount actually evidenced by the payment provider, at most
> once per payment, and never more than the fee being returned. The amount
> retained is shown on your refund statement.

Conditions restated by counsel: enters the Terms in this single C1.9 version
**before** the refund-policy flip; evidenced cost only; the client's own
refund is never reduced by it. This is **the last condition on activating the
refund policy** (handoff PART 3 table). Suggested placement: Section 12
("Deposits and payments") for the deposit lane and the new Section 13 for the
goods lane — the mechanics are identical (evidenced-cost retention, capped,
never doubled), so one clause covering both, cross-referenced from each
section, avoids saying the same rule twice with room to drift.

## A5 — Artist Terms: buyer-requested invoices are the artist's to issue

**Approved wording** (handoff PART 4, A5): "Add one line to the artist Terms:
buyer-requested invoices are the artist's to issue; Inklee supplies the
data." Full context: Inklee's own document is its fee invoice to the artist
(once the goods fee is non-zero); a buyer-requested invoice for the goods
sale itself is the artist's obligation as seller, and consumer sales need no
invoice unless requested or local law requires it.

**Not yet implemented anywhere** (no code surface — this is Terms-only).
Suggested line for the new Section 13:

> If a buyer requests an invoice for a goods purchase, issuing it is your
> responsibility as the seller. Inklee supplies the order data you need
> (items, prices, buyer-provided details) but does not issue buyer invoices
> for goods sales on your behalf.
